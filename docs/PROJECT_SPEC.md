## 2. Business or organisation

### 2.1 Public identity

- **Public project name:** CIC Handbook.
- **Working description:** A free information initiative that helps people understand Western Australian criminal injuries compensation and supports organisations that assist them.
- **Jurisdiction:** Western Australia.
- **Legal or operating entity:** `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- **Project owner and accountable publisher:** `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- **Formal non-profit status:** `[TO CONFIRM]`.
- **Government, court, or legal-service affiliation:** `[TO CONFIRM]`.

The website must not imply that CIC Handbook is a government agency, court, law firm, or authorised decision-maker unless that status is explicitly confirmed.

### 2.2 Information status

- The website provides general legal information, not legal advice.
- Information must be written in plain language and presented in a compassionate, trauma-informed manner.
- The website may direct users to official application channels, source documents, and support services.
- The website does not determine eligibility, assess applications, or make compensation decisions.

## 3. Website purpose

The website has two connected purposes:

1. **Individual pathway:** Help people seeking criminal injuries compensation understand the Western Australian process and work through it independently.
2. **Organisation pathway:** Provide training modules and supporting resources for organisations and workers who help others navigate the criminal injuries compensation process.

The intended outcomes are:

- reduce confusion about the Western Australian process;
- explain the stages, requirements, evidence, and possible next steps;
- help users locate official forms and application channels;
- connect users with support and legal-help resources; and
- equip organisations to provide informed, consistent assistance.

The website must not promise eligibility, a particular award, a processing time, or a successful outcome.

## 5. Primary action and user journeys

### 5.1 Primary site action

The homepage must first help a visitor select the appropriate pathway:

- **I’m seeking compensation** → begin the individual CIC guide.
- **I’m an organisation** → open the confirmed organisation pathway. Until
  training and resources are approved, its page displays “Coming soon”.

### 5.2 Individual journey

1. Select the individual pathway.
2. Enter the Western Australian CIC guide.
3. Navigate the guide by topic or process stage.
4. Follow links to official application material where appropriate.
5. Access support services or legal-help information when needed.

### 5.3 Organisation journey

1. Select the organisation pathway.
2. Open the confirmed `/for-organisations/` page.
3. Read the current “Coming soon” availability message.

Training content, supporting resources, module sequencing, authentication,
enrolment, progress tracking, assessment, certificates, and completion records
remain `[TO CONFIRM]`. They are not part of the current public journey.

### 5.4 Supporting journeys

- Search the handbook for a topic or phrase.
- Print or obtain a downloadable version of guide material.
- Move from the guide to support services.
- Leave the website for an official application or source document.

## 12. Responsive requirements

The website must work on mobile, tablet, laptop, and wide desktop layouts. Exact supported viewport widths and breakpoint values are `[TO CONFIRM]`.

Required responsive behaviour:

- replace desktop navigation with an accessible compact menu when it no longer fits;
- keep search and both audience pathways discoverable on small screens;
- stack the two homepage audience cards vertically when horizontal space is insufficient;
- convert multi-column value, resource, and footer sections to readable single-column or reduced-column layouts;
- move the long-guide sidebar into an accessible collapsible or in-flow navigation pattern;
- prevent content, tables, forms, images, and controls from causing page-level horizontal scrolling;
- allow data tables to scroll within a labelled container or use an equivalent small-screen presentation;
- stack form fields and actions where required;
- preserve a logical reading and focus order independent of visual layout;
- keep tap targets separated and usable;
- support text enlargement and browser zoom without lost content or functionality; and
- provide appropriately sized and cropped imagery without placing essential text inside images.

The supplied homepage reference is a desktop reference. Mobile and tablet visual designs are `[TO CONFIRM]`.

## 14. Accessibility requirements

The formal conformance target is `[TO CONFIRM]`.

Regardless of the final target, the implementation must:

- use semantic landmarks, headings, lists, buttons, links, tables, and form controls;
- provide keyboard access to navigation, menus, search, accordions, guide links, and forms;
- provide visible keyboard focus;
- include a mechanism to bypass repeated navigation;
- maintain meaningful focus order when menus or dialogs open;
- not use colour as the sole way to communicate meaning or status;
- meet approved text and interactive-component contrast requirements `[TO CONFIRM: formal ratios through selected standard]`;
- provide alternative text for informative imagery and empty alternative text for decorative imagery;
- avoid embedding essential instructions or copy solely in images;
- give controls accessible names that match their visible purpose;
- use descriptive external-link and telephone-link labels;
- preserve content at text enlargement and browser zoom;
- provide accessible error, loading, empty, and success states;
- avoid autoplaying sound, video, or distracting motion;
- respect reduced-motion preferences if animation is introduced;
- present content warnings before sensitive material;
- use plain language and avoid unnecessarily graphic descriptions;
- keep emergency and support information easy to locate; and
- ensure downloadable documents are accessible or provide an equivalent accessible HTML version.

Accessibility testing must include keyboard-only use and representative screen-reader testing. The assistive-technology and browser matrix is `[TO CONFIRM]`.

## 15. Explicit exclusions

Unless a later approved specification states otherwise, CIC Handbook does not:

- provide legal advice or legal representation;
- determine whether a person is eligible;
- assess, lodge, manage, or decide a compensation application;
- guarantee compensation, a particular amount, timing, or outcome;
- accept payments or charge for a service;
- collect claim evidence or sensitive application material;
- replace official government, court, police, health, legal, or emergency services;
- provide CIC guidance for jurisdictions outside Western Australia;
- operate user accounts, authentication, learner profiles, progress tracking, assessments, or certificates;
- operate a newsletter, contact form, analytics platform, CRM, LMS, or social-media integration until each is confirmed;
- publish unverified services, claims, prices, locations, contact details, or external links; or
