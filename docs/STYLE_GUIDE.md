# CIC Handbook Visual Style Guide

## 1. Purpose, scope and authority

This document defines the visual and creative direction for the CIC Handbook website. It translates the requirements in [`PROJECT_SPEC.md`](PROJECT_SPEC.md), the foundations in [`DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md), the supplied [`brand-board.png`](reference/brand-board.png), and the approved homepage references in [`docs/reference/`](reference/) into practical art-direction and composition rules.

This guide does not:

- replace or amend the Design System;
- define editorial voice or detailed writing conventions;
- approve website pages, components, integrations or conditional features;
- authorise a logo, image, icon or font file that has not been supplied; or
- provide website code.

The current request defines `STYLE_GUIDE.md` as a visual and creative-direction document. That direct instruction takes precedence over the earlier statement in `PROJECT_SPEC.md` that associated this file with editorial voice.

### 1.1 Source-status legend

- **Confirmed direction** — required by the Project Specification or explicitly established by the Design System.
- **Visual interpretation** — a practical creative decision inferred from the confirmed requirements and brand board. It must not be presented as an exact value extracted from the reference.
- **`[TO CONFIRM]`** — a missing decision or asset. It is not authorised for implementation until confirmed.

### 1.2 Source precedence

Where sources differ, apply this order:

1. Direct decisions from the project owner.
2. `PROJECT_SPEC.md`.
3. `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`.
4. The visual reference.

`docs/reference/` contains the brand board and approved desktop homepage references. The hero reference confirms the broad coastal composition, protected left-aligned copy, lower audience chooser and balanced two-card treatment. The support-principles and basics references confirm their respective information hierarchy and desktop composition. Exact colours, fonts, radii and shadows continue to come from the Design System rather than being sampled from raster references.

## 2. Overall visual personality

The interface should feel like a calm, carefully organised handbook that happens to be digital.

| Quality | How it must appear in the interface | What it must not become |
|---|---|---|
| Calm | Stable layouts, restrained colour, predictable navigation, unhurried spacing and minimal motion | Empty, passive or so subdued that actions are difficult to find |
| Grounded | Warm neutral surfaces, Western Australian imagery, tactile editorial details and clear alignment | Rustic decoration, tourism advertising or decorative references unrelated to the content |
| Compassionate | Respectful photography, content warnings, supportive language placement and easy access to help | Pitying imagery, sentimental clichés, forced reassurance or infantilising presentation |
| Instructional | Visible hierarchy, short introductions, ordered steps, meaningful callouts and clear next actions | Dense legal-document formatting, unexplained diagrams or gamified progress |
| Trustworthy | Accurate visual hierarchy, restrained emphasis, readable source material and consistent interaction patterns | A government seal, court identity, law-firm aesthetic or suggestion that CIC Handbook decides claims |
| Human | Natural light, approachable typography, plain layouts and imagery that centres dignity | Cold clinical interfaces, generic corporate handshakes or artificial crisis scenes |

Avoid using words such as “modern”, “clean” or “professional” as standalone design goals. If those qualities are discussed, they must refer to observable decisions such as consistent alignment, limited type styles, restrained elevation, uncluttered reading surfaces and accessible controls.

## 3. Desired emotional impression

**Confirmed direction:** the website must support people engaging with serious and potentially distressing information in a compassionate, trauma-informed way.

The visual journey should create four impressions in order:

1. **Orientation:** the visitor can quickly understand what the site is, its Western Australian scope and that it provides general information rather than legal advice.
2. **Agency:** the two audience pathways are equally visible, and the visitor can choose a route without being judged or pressured.
3. **Steadiness:** the guide breaks a complex process into manageable sections with a clear reading order and visible ways to get help.
4. **Support:** assistance and emergency information remain easy to find without overwhelming every page with alarm styling.

The interface should never use:

- urgency as a conversion tactic;
- celebratory language or visuals for legal-process steps;
- fear, alarm or graphic imagery to command attention;
- pity as the basis of emotional connection;
- a visual promise of eligibility, compensation or a successful outcome; or
- progress styling that suggests CIC Handbook is tracking or deciding an application.

## 4. Layout style

### 4.1 Core composition

**Confirmed direction:** use the Design System’s centred containers and preserve a narrow reading measure for long-form content.

- Use the 768px reading container for the WA Guide, support information and other sustained reading.
- Use the 1200px content container for navigation, audience cards, resource groups and standard page sections.
- Reserve the 1440px wide container for a justified editorial image composition or broad visual section; do not stretch body copy to fill it.
- Align headings, copy, controls and cards to a discernible grid. Avoid small alignment changes that make adjacent sections feel unrelated.
- Give each section one dominant message, task or decision.
- Keep both homepage audience pathways equal in prominence, target size and discoverability.
- Use asymmetry only for purposeful editorial compositions, such as one large image balanced by a smaller text or image group. Do not use asymmetry where it disrupts reading or focus order.

### 4.2 Page-specific direction

**Visual interpretation:**

- **Home:** use a full-width Warm Ivory hero with protected left-aligned copy, a right-weighted coastal image that fades into the page surface, and the two audience choices grouped prominently below. On smaller screens, move the image into an in-flow 16:9 panel and stack the cards in individual-then-organisation order. Continue below the hero with principles, then a constrained two-column introductory panel that pairs the basics pathway with calm editorial imagery and stacks content before imagery on smaller screens.
- **WA Guide:** prioritise the reading column and topic navigation. Visual devices should clarify sequence, definitions, warnings and next steps rather than decorate long passages.
- **Support Services:** place emergency and high-priority support information before editorial imagery or spacious promotional composition.
- **Organisation pathway:** use orderly module and resource groups, but do not imply accounts, completion tracking, assessment or certification unless those features are confirmed.

## 5. Use of whitespace

Warm Ivory and White negative space are active parts of the visual identity, not leftover areas.

### 5.1 Whitespace rules

**Confirmed direction and Design System application:**

- Use the 4px spacing scale and the documented container gutters.
- Keep generous space between unrelated sections and tighter space between a heading, its introduction and its action.
- Use 48–96px section separation according to importance and viewport size.
- Use 16–24px internal card and callout spacing.
- Keep enough space around callouts that they are recognisable as a change of context.
- Maintain a continuous reading rhythm in the guide; do not insert large decorative gaps between paragraphs that belong to one explanation.
- Let the page background show between major groups rather than filling every area with a card or tint.

### 5.2 Whitespace must not

- push emergency, support or primary pathway information below unnecessary decoration;
- create the appearance of missing content;
- separate a label from its field or a heading from its content;
- produce large empty desktop areas simply because content is short;
- reduce scanability by placing every sentence in a separate block; or
- replace a visible divider or heading when a relationship would otherwise be unclear.

## 6. Section composition

### 6.1 Standard section anatomy

**Visual interpretation:** a typical section should contain only the elements required for its task, normally in this order:

1. Optional short overline or context label.
2. Concise heading.
3. Brief explanatory copy.
4. One focused action, content group or decision.
5. Optional supporting image, callout or reference material.

Do not include every element merely to make sections look uniform. A long-form guide section may need only a heading and content, while an audience-choice section needs concise descriptions and explicit actions.

### 6.2 Composition patterns

- Alternate open neutral sections with occasional tinted or card-based groups; do not make every section a coloured panel.
- Use a full-width surface only when it establishes a clear page phase or high-level grouping. Keep its content inside a constrained container.
- Use callouts to interrupt reading only when they identify a helpful tip, important warning, next step or contextual fact.
- Use dividers sparingly. Prefer spacing and headings for hierarchy.
- Keep one primary action per decision area. Secondary actions must be visually quieter.
- Use process steps only when the content is genuinely sequential.
- Pair every semantic tint with a written heading and icon or other non-colour cue.

### 6.3 Process and progress displays

The brand board includes a four-step process and pagination treatment. These may guide editorial navigation only.

They must not imply:

- confirmed eligibility;
- claim submission or application status;
- progress stored against a person;
- training completion;
- assessment results;
- certification; or
- a guaranteed linear path through a legal process.

Personalised progress behaviour remains **`[TO CONFIRM]`** and is currently outside scope.

## 7. Typography application

### 7.1 Roles

| Use | Direction | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Page and section headings | Manrope 600 or 700, normally sentence case | **Confirmed direction** |
| Body copy and explanatory content | Inter 400 | **Confirmed direction** |
| Buttons, labels and navigation | Inter 500 | **Confirmed direction** |
| Short overlines | Manrope or Inter at the approved small size, uppercase only when brief and legible | **Visual interpretation** |
| Decorative or alternative typeface | Do not use | **Confirmed direction** |

Neither Manrope nor Inter is currently loaded in the repository. Font source, licensing and delivery remain **`[TO CONFIRM]`**. Layouts must tolerate the documented fallback stacks and must not depend on exact line breaks from an unavailable font.

### 7.2 Hierarchy and rhythm

- Apply the H1–H6 sizes, weights, line heights and tracking from `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`.
- Use one semantic H1 for the principal page title.
- Keep display headings short enough to remain readable at the documented 40–64px range.
- Keep long-form text within approximately 60–75 characters per line.
- Use the 18px large-body style for introductions, not for all body content.
- Use 16px body text as the default minimum reading size.
- Use 12px captions only for non-essential metadata.
- Do not use Burgundy for paragraphs or long text.
- Do not use all capitals for body copy, instructions, warnings or buttons.
- Do not use font weight or size alone to communicate an error, warning or required action.

## 8. Colour balance

### 8.1 Visual hierarchy

**Confirmed direction:**

1. **Warm Ivory and White** form the dominant page and card surfaces.
2. **Soft Grey** separates neutral groups without making the page feel segmented into many competing panels.
3. **Midnight Navy** carries most headings, body text, line icons and structural emphasis.
4. **Deep Burgundy** identifies primary actions, selected emphasis and important content.
5. **Sage Mist, Dusty Rose, Sand and Dusty Blue** appear as local background tints for specific categories.

The tints are not four alternate page themes. Use them only where their meaning is clear and useful.

### 8.2 Balance rules

- Keep the majority of every content-heavy view on Warm Ivory, White or Soft Grey.
- Use Burgundy in small, deliberate areas so a primary action remains easy to locate.
- Avoid placing several large Burgundy regions close together.
- Use no more than one dominant semantic tint within a single callout or card group.
- Do not distribute every tint evenly simply to make a page more colourful.
- Use Midnight Navy or Deep Burgundy on light tints according to the approved contrast pairings.
- Use Slate for normal-sized text only on White, Warm Ivory or Soft Grey.
- Never use Midnight Navy directly against Deep Burgundy for essential text or controls.
- Never use one light tint as text on another light surface.
- Do not introduce colours from the current legacy stylesheet into new visual direction.

The organisation green, “Next step” blue and “Did you know?” olive foregrounds shown in the board have no approved exact values. Use the Design System’s Midnight Navy interim treatment until they are confirmed.

## 9. Image and photography direction

### 9.1 Subject matter

**Confirmed direction from the brand board:**

- Western Australian landscapes, coastlines and recognisable environmental character;
- calm, supportive human interactions;
- notebooks, printed guidance and tactile handbook materials;
- warm natural light; and
- uncluttered editorial compositions with a clear subject.

### 9.2 Selection and art direction

**Visual interpretation:**

- Select images that communicate dignity, steadiness and practical support rather than distress.
- Use believable environments and interactions. Avoid subjects performing exaggerated emotion for the camera.
- Represent people respectfully and avoid using identity as a decorative shorthand for vulnerability.
- Keep backgrounds quiet enough that the subject remains legible at responsive crops.
- Use landscape imagery to establish Western Australian context, not to imply a service location or statewide physical presence.
- Use 16:9 crops for broad editorial or hero imagery and 3:2 for supporting images, as defined in the Design System.
- Preserve a meaningful focal point when an image is cropped.
- Keep colour grading warm and natural. Skin, landscape and brand colours must not be distorted.
- Use a protected solid surface for essential text rather than placing it directly over complex photography.
- Provide useful alternative text when an image conveys information and empty alternative text when it is decorative.

### 9.3 Avoid

- graphic depictions of violence, injury or distress;
- staged crisis, grief or fear;
- police tape, gavels, handcuffs, courtrooms or similar generic legal clichés unless the content specifically and responsibly requires them;
- generic corporate handshakes;
- images that imply a successful claim or guaranteed outcome;
- high-saturation filters, heavy vignettes or dramatic colour casts;
- essential instructions embedded in an image;
- imagery that competes with emergency or support information; and
- unlicensed or unapproved assets.

The final image library, photography source, licensing, consent and attribution are **`[TO CONFIRM]`**.

## 10. Decorative graphic treatment

### 10.1 Approved direction

The board demonstrates:

- thin line icons;
- rounded line geometry;
- checklist and document imagery;
- paths and journey diagrams;
- supportive-conversation illustrations;
- Western Australian map outlines;
- dotted fields;
- arches; and
- large partial circles using pale tints.

### 10.2 Application

**Visual interpretation:**

- Use Midnight Navy as the default line colour and Deep Burgundy for restrained emphasis.
- Use approved pale tints as limited supporting fills.
- Keep motifs peripheral to the main reading column.
- Use no more than one prominent decorative motif family within a section.
- Crop or omit motifs on small screens when they compete with content.
- Keep decorative patterns low contrast and away from text, controls and focus indicators.
- Use a graphic to reinforce an existing idea, never as the only source of meaning.
- Keep icon stroke, corner and detail density consistent across the site.
- Hide purely decorative graphics from assistive technology.

Do not:

- extract icons, logos or illustrations from the raster board;
- mix filled, three-dimensional, photoreal and line-icon styles;
- use seals, crests or symbols that suggest government or court authority;
- create decorative background noise behind long-form text;
- use large Burgundy patterns that introduce alarm; or
- add an unapproved colour for illustration variety.

Approved production SVG assets and the icon/illustration source library are **`[TO CONFIRM]`**.

## 11. Button and call-to-action treatment

### 11.1 Hierarchy

| Treatment | Use | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | One principal action within a decision area | Deep Burgundy fill with Warm Ivory or White text and trailing line arrow |
| Secondary | Alternative or lower-priority navigation | White, Warm Ivory or transparent surface with Deep Burgundy text and border |
| Text link | Inline or tertiary navigation | Descriptive text with a clear non-colour hover/focus treatment |
| Organisation action | Organisation pathway | Use the approved interim Navy treatment until an exact organisation green is confirmed |

### 11.2 CTA rules

- Use specific verb-led labels such as “Start the guide”, “View support services” or “Access training modules” when those destinations are confirmed.
- Do not use “Click here”, “Learn more” without context, or language that promises an outcome.
- Use one primary action per decision area.
- Keep the arrow subordinate to the label; it does not replace descriptive text.
- Preserve the Design System’s minimum 44px target, 8px radius, Inter 500 label and visible focus treatment.
- Use brief movement or elevation for hover feedback instead of inventing a darker Burgundy.
- Keep actions visually stable while loading or processing.
- Do not use an icon-only primary CTA.
- Do not make an entire complex content card interactive if it contains competing links.
- Do not use pill-shaped primary buttons; reserve full rounding for pagination or compact controls.

Newsletter, contact, account, LMS and social actions remain conditional features. Their appearance is not authorised until their scope and behaviour are confirmed.

## 12. Card treatment

### 12.1 General direction

Cards should make a discrete choice or content group easier to understand. They should not become the default container for every paragraph.

**Confirmed and interpreted treatment:**

- White surface.
- Moderate 12px radius.
- Restrained Navy-derived shadow.
- Concise heading, short supporting copy and no more than one dominant action.
- Optional line icon in a pale circular tint.
- Internal spacing from the Design System’s 20–24px card range.

The exact base-card border token is affected by the contradiction recorded in section 19. Until the Design System is corrected, the intended visual direction is a faint decorative card boundary with restrained elevation, while essential form controls require a perceptible solid boundary.

### 12.2 Variants

- **Audience cards:** give the individual and organisation pathways equal size, hierarchy and visual weight. Different accents may identify the pathway, but the label, icon and copy must carry the distinction without colour.
- **Resource or training cards:** show a clear title, purpose and confirmed destination. Do not display completion or enrolment states that are not implemented.
- **Callouts:** use Helpful tip, Important, Next step and Did you know treatments only for their stated purpose.
- **Emergency information:** do not rely on a standard promotional card treatment. Give it immediate placement, a direct heading and clear action.

### 12.3 Avoid

- nested cards;
- card grids containing long prose that belongs in the reading flow;
- several competing links inside one card;
- strong black shadows or coloured glows;
- excessive hover lift;
- equal elevation on every page element;
- relying on tint alone to identify a callout; and
- using cards to imitate a dashboard when no account or tracking system exists.

## 13. Desktop design behaviour

**Visual interpretation based on the desktop board and confirmed layout requirements:**

- Centre the page within the documented containers rather than stretching content across a wide display.
- Keep the primary navigation, search and both audience pathways easy to locate.
- Present the audience choices as a balanced two-column pair where space permits.
- Use multi-column value, resource or callout groups only when each item remains scannable and similar in content length.
- Use asymmetric image mosaics sparingly for editorial interest; they must not dictate the page structure.
- Keep long-form guide copy within the reading container even when navigation or related content sits beside it.
- Let section spacing and surface changes define major phases rather than adding many divider lines.
- Avoid enlarging every component merely because more viewport width is available.
- Preserve source order so the layout remains logical when columns collapse.
- Keep emergency and support content in the primary scan path.

The exact supported desktop viewport matrix remains **`[TO CONFIRM]`**.

## 14. Mobile design behaviour

The brand board provides no mobile design. Every rule in this section is a **Visual interpretation** derived from the responsive requirements and Design System.

- Use the documented 16px base gutter and keep body text at 16px or larger.
- Replace the desktop navigation with a compact, keyboard-operable pattern when it no longer fits.
- Keep search and both audience pathways discoverable without requiring exploration of unrelated content.
- Stack audience cards vertically in the same logical order as desktop.
- Stack multi-column values, resources and callouts into a clear reading order.
- Allow primary actions to use the available width when that improves label readability and target size.
- Move long-guide navigation into an accessible in-flow or collapsible pattern.
- Simplify image mosaics to one strong image or a short vertical sequence.
- Crop, reduce or omit decorative motifs when they interfere with content.
- Reduce large section gaps before reducing typography below its documented minimum.
- Keep controls at least 44px high and separated from adjacent targets.
- Ensure tables and other wide content do not cause page-level horizontal scrolling.
- Preserve logical reading and focus order independently of the desktop composition.
- Avoid sticky elements that consume excessive vertical space or obscure content.

Exact supported mobile and tablet widths and final visual designs remain **`[TO CONFIRM]`**.

## 15. Animation and motion approach

The reference is static and confirms no animation. Motion is optional and must improve orientation or interaction feedback.

### 15.1 Permitted motion

The following timings are **Visual interpretations**:

| Interaction | Duration | Direction |
|---|---:|---|
| Button, link or field feedback | 150–200ms | Small colour-independent emphasis, shadow change or arrow movement |
| Disclosure, menu or accordion | 200–250ms | Controlled reveal that preserves focus and reading order |
| Card hover | 150–200ms | At most a subtle elevation or 1–2px movement when the card is genuinely interactive |

### 15.2 Motion rules

- Keep easing gentle and decelerating rather than springy or elastic.
- Do not animate essential content into availability.
- Do not delay access to emergency, support, guide or legal-information content.
- Do not use autoplaying video, sound, parallax, looping decoration, bouncing controls, dramatic page reveals or animated counters.
- Do not use motion to imply progress, success or completion.
- Stop or remove non-essential translation and reveal effects when reduced motion is requested.
- Ensure disclosures remain understandable when animation is removed.
- Keep focus indicators immediate rather than animated into view.

## 16. Visual accessibility considerations

### 16.1 Colour and contrast

- Follow the exact pairings and contrast evidence in `DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`.
- Use Midnight Navy or Deep Burgundy for normal-sized text on light tints.
- Use Slate normal text only on White, Warm Ivory or Soft Grey.
- Do not rely on colour alone for pathway, callout, validation, status or navigation meaning.
- Test any transparency, image overlay or future semantic colour separately.
- Do not claim formal WCAG conformance until the project target and audit process are confirmed.

### 16.2 Readability and interaction

- Keep default body copy at 16px or larger and within the reading measure.
- Provide visible keyboard focus that is not obscured by shadows or decorative graphics.
- Maintain a minimum 44px interactive target.
- Use real visible labels for fields; placeholder text may provide an example but cannot be the only label.
- Keep link and button labels descriptive.
- Preserve a logical semantic and focus order when layouts reflow.
- Ensure content remains available at 320px viewport width and 200% browser zoom without page-level horizontal scrolling.
- Never place essential text over an uncontrolled photographic background.

### 16.3 Images, icons and motion

- Give informative imagery useful alternative text and decorative imagery empty alternative text.
- Pair icons with written labels when they communicate an action, category or status.
- Hide decorative line art and patterns from assistive technology.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Do not use flashing, rapid, looping or surprise movement.

### 16.4 Trauma-informed visual accessibility

- Keep navigation and page structure predictable.
- Present a content warning before sensitive material.
- Keep emergency and support pathways easy to locate.
- Avoid imagery or decoration that could be distressing, sensational or ambiguous.
- Do not trap a user in a staged flow; allow direct navigation to relevant sections and support.
- Use calm visual emphasis without concealing the seriousness of important information.

The formal accessibility standard and assistive-technology test matrix remain **`[TO CONFIRM]`**.

## 17. Design dos

- Do let Warm Ivory, White and Soft Grey provide the dominant visual canvas.
- Do use Midnight Navy for most reading text and structural line work.
- Do reserve Burgundy for primary actions and important emphasis.
- Do give the individual and organisation pathways equal visibility and visual weight.
- Do use concise headings, narrow reading measures and ordered content groups.
- Do keep support and emergency information in the primary scan path.
- Do use specific, verb-led CTA labels.
- Do maintain one coherent line-icon and illustration family.
- Do pair every colour category with a heading, icon or text label.
- Do use WA imagery accurately and sparingly.
- Do preserve whitespace without breaking the guide’s reading flow.
- Do use cards only for genuine choices, resources, modules or bounded callouts.
- Do test responsive crop, text wrapping, focus order, zoom and reduced motion.
- Do distinguish general information from official application channels.
- Do mark unresolved assets or treatments `[TO CONFIRM]` rather than approximating them.

## 18. Design don’ts

- Do not make the site resemble a government agency, court, law firm or compensation decision-maker.
- Do not use the CIC WA raster lockup as an approved CIC Handbook production logo.
- Do not sample unlabelled green, blue, olive or gradient colours from the board.
- Do not introduce legacy stylesheet colours as new brand colours.
- Do not use graphic, staged or sensational distress imagery.
- Do not use tourism imagery without a clear informational or contextual purpose.
- Do not place essential copy inside or directly over complex imagery.
- Do not make one audience pathway appear secondary.
- Do not turn every section into a card grid.
- Do not nest cards or use dashboard patterns for unconfirmed account features.
- Do not use strong black shadows, coloured glows or excessive elevation.
- Do not use Burgundy for long paragraphs or large alarm-like backgrounds.
- Do not use colour, iconography or motion as the sole source of meaning.
- Do not use progress graphics to imply eligibility, claim status or stored completion.
- Do not use autoplay, parallax, looping decoration or dramatic entrance sequences.
- Do not shrink body text or targets to preserve a desktop arrangement.
- Do not let decorative motifs cover content, controls or focus indicators.
- Do not visually present a conditional form or integration as available before it is approved.
